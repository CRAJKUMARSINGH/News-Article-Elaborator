---
name: Editorial evidence guardrail
description: The trust and wording rule for investigative report and presentation outputs.
---

Investigative outputs should distinguish established source facts, reported observations, unresolved concerns, and recommendations in both wording and visual treatment. Never turn a concern into a proven allegation through a headline, callout, or slide takeaway.

**Why:** The source material mixes direct observations, operational concerns, and corruption-risk language. Clear separation improves reader trust and prevents the generated report from overstating what the supplied evidence establishes.

**How to apply:** When adding or revising report content, preserve the evidence status in the copy and in the interface. Keep the chronology factual, label unresolved claims, and phrase recommendations as corrective actions rather than conclusions about wrongdoing.
